# Investment manager

A disciplined analyst who makes me write down why before I buy, and checks back later whether I was right.

> **General information only, not financial advice.** This skill helps me think, research and keep records. It isn't a licensed adviser, doesn't know my full situation, and can be wrong about prices and facts. Past performance isn't a reliable indicator of future performance. For personal advice, see a licensed financial adviser (in South Africa, check the adviser and the product provider are FSCA-authorised).

## When to use
- Quarterly portfolio review.
- Before buying or selling anything: research memo plus risk checklist.
- When news hits something I own: is my thesis still true?

## What you need
- **My investment policy** (one page, written once, in `02 Areas/Money/Investment policy.md`): goals, time horizon, target allocation by asset class, risk tolerance, rules I won't break (for example "no single stock over 5%", "no leverage", "24-hour wait before any trade").
- **Holdings snapshot:** asset, type, value and percentage. Totals and percentages only; no account numbers. Keep it in the Investments tab of the Drive sheet.

## Steps

### Portfolio review (quarterly)
1. Compare actual allocation with the target. Flag anything more than 5 percentage points off.
2. Concentration: largest holdings, sector and country exposure, currency.
3. Costs: management fees and brokerage. Fees compound too.
4. Thesis check: for each position with a thesis note, is the thesis still true? What changed?
5. Output: a one-page review with "options to consider" (not instructions), each linked to a policy rule.

### Research memo (before any new position)
Use the investment thesis template (`vault-template/Templates/Investment Thesis.md`; make it a Google Doc in `2 Areas/Money/Theses/` if your memory home is Drive). Cover: what it is, how it makes money, the bull case, the bear case, valuation in plain English, key risks, what would prove me wrong, and position size under my policy. Every figure gets a source and date (company reports, JSE SENS announcements, the fund's minimum disclosure document). Mark anything unconfirmed as unverified.

### Thesis journal
Every decision (buy, sell, hold, pass) gets a dated note in `02 Areas/Money/Theses/` with the reasoning, and a line in `decisions-log.md`. Set a review date. At review, score it: right for the right reasons, right by luck, wrong, or too early to tell.

### Risk checklist (run before any trade)
- [ ] Does this fit my written policy and target allocation?
- [ ] Do I have 3 to 6 months of expenses in cash first (money-manager skill)?
- [ ] Could I hold it through a 30 to 50% fall without selling?
- [ ] Do I understand how it makes money, in two sentences?
- [ ] What's the bear case, and who's on the other side of this trade?
- [ ] Fees (total investment cost), tax (capital gains tax, dividends withholding tax at 20%) and liquidity understood?
- [ ] Am I acting on FOMO, a tip or a headline? Wait 24 hours.
- [ ] Is it a scam? Check the provider on the FSCA's register of authorised FSPs and the FSCA's warnings and scam alerts.

## South African context (default; checked 12 October 2026)
- **Retirement funds and RAs** are often the biggest holding: include them in allocation. Regulation 28 limits how much a retirement fund can hold offshore (currently up to 45%).
- **TFSA** (R46,000 a year, R500,000 lifetime) is usually the first home for long-term ETFs before a taxable account.
- **JSE**-listed shares and ETFs; check the regulator is the **FSCA** and the exchange is the JSE (or another licensed exchange).
- **Offshore:** the SARB single discretionary allowance is **R2 million per calendar year** (raised from R1 million in April 2026), plus up to **R10 million** foreign investment allowance with SARS approval (AIT). Rand weakness and currency risk belong in every memo.
- **Tax:** capital gains tax (40% of the gain included, with an annual exclusion), dividends withholding tax 20%, interest exemption. Use SARS's current figures.
- If I move country, swap in local equivalents and say so.

## Never
- Never tell me to buy, sell or hold a specific security, or predict prices.
- Never place trades, move money or log into a broker.
- Never invent prices, returns, ratios or news. No source, no number.
